Guess the CS2 Skin

Like GuessTheGame, but for Counter-Strike weapon finishes.

SvelteKit · Cloudflare Workers · 2025 · play it

A CS2 skin is shown zoomed so far in that it's a few pixels of pattern. You guess. Wrong, or stuck, and it reveals a little more — five stages, one guess each. Get it early and you've genuinely earned it; get it at stage five and you've at least learned a finish you didn't know.

Play it →

How it's built

SvelteKit on Cloudflare Workers. Skins, per-stage image references and player progress live in D1; the five crops of each skin live in R2. Round state rides in a cookie, so there's no account and nothing to sign up for.

The five stages aren't generated on the fly — each skin is cropped once, offline, by a small editor built for the job, and the five results are stored as rows in skin_images. Deciding where to crop is a judgement call per skin (the interesting few pixels are rarely in the middle), so it's a human step by design rather than a failure to automate one.

The cheating problem

Any guessing game where the answer reaches the browser is solvable with devtools, and a zoom-reveal game is worse than most: if all five images ship up front, stage five is one network tab away.

So the image endpoint checks progress server-side and refuses. Ask for a stage beyond the one you've earned and you get a 403 — not a hidden element, an actual refusal, unless you've already solved that skin. The answer isn't sitting in the database in plaintext either; skins stores an encrypted_name alongside the display name. Guesses are checked on the Worker.

Getting the names right

There's no official CS2 skin API, so the data comes from a scraper that works case by case — Dreams & Nightmares, Fracture, Kilowatt and the rest — into per-case JSON. Names turned out to be the fiddly part: a finish has a formal name and whatever players actually call it, and a quiz that accepts only one of those is infuriating to play. The guess box autocompletes against the real list, so you're choosing from what exists rather than fighting a spelling.
