"The Design of Everyday Things" is a book on human-centered design in everyday objects. It's a great book, I think, I'm not sure, cause I've neither read it, nor own it. But I would like to.
Since everything has to be about AI, we're going to talk about AI slop and how not everything has to be about AI.

Everything, according to critics, started with the design of the original iPhone. Sleek, futuristic, clean, beautiful. Unfortunately since then the future has been inspired a little too much by it. Phones lack "innovation", they're not daring enough. Inefficient even.

Is it impossible to give it a little more personality? Are the hardware requirements just too limiting to design? I think there's enough breathing room.

![[design-eras-memphis-y2k-frutiger-aero-flat.png|Collage of four design eras, each with its own Pepsi logo, consoles and computer screens: colourful Memphis Design (1984–1997), chrome Y2K (1997–2004), glossy blue-green Frutiger Aero (2004–2013), and plain white Flat Design (2013–2023)]]

https://www.reddit.com/r/y2kaesthetic/comments/12tbogq/eras_of_aesthetics/


*But breathing, to metal, concrete and plastic, is offensive.* Ok, let's talk cars: Inoffensive, grey, boring, soulless. Same as the phone really.
Is a Tesla ugly? No! It's clean, sleek, shiny. Is it pretty? Yeah, I guess?

What about these new cars? I'm certain they'll be deemed either UGLY or well, "dope af". What is certain is that they'll eradicate indifference.

![[renault-5-turbo-3e.png|Renault 5 Turbo 3E, a boxy electric hatchback in bright yellow with black and white panels, wide flared wheel arches and R5 TURBO 3E lettering down the side|542]]
https://press24.net/news/37546972/renault-5-turbo-kehrt-2025-als-elektro-auto-mit-500-ps-zur-ck

For some even before the shape the color will be shocking: No black, white, grey, anthracite, silver, ivory, pearl, ashen, charcoal. Blue or red for daredevils.
What drives the fear of getting tired of color so much that living in boredom right away is preferred? *Fear of boredom* is supposed to inform my decisions?

I'm a 70s/80s fan. I'm not against new things, I'm not ranting against planned obsolescence, low quality or a specific aesthetic. We're in fast cycles, but nostalgia throwbacks without depth and inoffensive design isn't it. I don't even mind proper design era cycles setting in.

But I very much mind inoffensive, slop-oriented output. And now that AI can produce this "slop" en masse it's under scrutiny.
NPC like behavior, being a shell of an employee, sending out low-effort templates, designing a microwave with 10 buttons requiring a cryptography expert to decipher isn't new. I wish there was a book about that.
Low-effort slop isn't an AI thing. If anything, I hope the amount of slop will make those who eat it just because it’s available and doesn’t taste bad finally stop.
