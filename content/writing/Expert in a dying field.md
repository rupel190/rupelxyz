How does it feel to be an expert in a dying field, asked in the song of the same name by The Beths.

Music gear never had the chance to get outdated the same way as technology. A 20 year old synthesizer is still viable. Try using 20 year old software. (No, I'm not making fun of GIMP). The [[UX of Music Gear|UX of Music Gear]] is a hidden discipline of UX without the Nielsen-top-10-UX or learnt expectations. It seemingly emerges from nothing, and it still works. The field tolerates radically different interaction models simultaneously. It has colors and knobs, often no screens, is described with terms like "menu diving". And there's a crowd for each and every device.

How often did music gear die? It was supposed to be: New devices replacing old ones, UX being unusable, one device being capable of everything replacing all other devices. Presets didn't kill it. Samples didn't kill it. AI music didn't kill it.
Google didn't acquire and shut it down, Microsoft didn't force an update, Apple didn't make it artificially slow. It's an offline box, loyal after all these years.

It lacks the orthodoxy of regular tech, often software. No convergence, no unified interaction model, but diversity. If there's no one right way, the new way can't be right.

And what's cool: There'll always be someone your age keeping the thing you too love alive.
