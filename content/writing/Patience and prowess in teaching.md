I'm a passionate autodidact. Back in school there was no space for me to be patient with myself. The further I could liberate myself of those structures the better I did. I became patient with myself.
But now I've noticed something odd: A shift in impatience when I try to explain something I already know to others. So I dug into it.


When you start learning a skill, depending on your perspective it either feels like an insurmountable mountain, or  the deep sea. With experience you notice the relevant parts usually condense down to a pond - you can skip a lot.
You dive straight in, or go for the theory first, then hit the first wall. Freshly motivated you remove the obstacles. Eventually you move more freely, parts get easy, others stay hidden away. You poke at them, uneasily.

That is where you start noticing something: Part of the skill has already gone into muscle memory. You wouldn't even think about it anymore, you wanna *flow* into it. Then you get stuck. You find your way out, but it's super hard. A minute ago you were breezing through all the material, now you're back on day one. It's a non-linear path of course. 
But now you're stuck in between. Part of your brain works in the muscle memory mode, another has to research and try every little step, again and again. You *narrate* the steps to yourself. Ultimately, if you stick to it and earn proficiency, the muscle memory will take over the skill completely.

But what will have happened by then? The foundation - the parts that became second nature - you don't remember narrating those. Did you ever? You're in deep. So deep that your narration won't get back out. The inner teacher drowning, _only clawing back with **patience**_.
